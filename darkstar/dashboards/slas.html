Self-Service Delivery

How much of PE's delivered work comes through the self-service skills, how fast those requests close, and how long merge requests take to land. All hours are business hours.

Self-service impact

About this panel

A label means the request came through a self-service skill; a Claude footer on the MR means the code was agent-written, whoever filed the ticket. They overlap but are not the same, so the share card reports both.

Delivery turnaround by month

About this panel

Self-service requests grouped by the month they were created. A single blended figure over the whole window libels current performance while the team is improving fast. The bar is the share closed inside one working day — the p50 range is wide enough that plotting it linearly would bury the recent months. Widen the lookback to see earlier months.

Agent share of delivery

About this panel

Every merge request merged that month, split by whether its description carries a Claude footer. MRs naming no Jira issue are counted too — the question is what share of PE's whole output the agent writes, not what share of the ticketed subset.

This is the panel that carries the self-service argument. Measured head to head, agent-written and hand-written MRs merge at the same speed — p50 0.3h against 0.1h across 424 and 1046 MRs, the same engineers in both groups — because merging is already minutes for everyone. What changed is volume: a rising share at flat headcount is capacity bought, not latency traded.

The month in progress is marked partial. Its counts are a part-month and its share is not settled. A trailing +n? counts MRs merged that month whose description the crawl has not read yet — authorship unknown, so they stay out of the share rather than being scored as hand-written.

MR turnaround

About this panel

Every merged MR the ingest attributes, ready → merged, slowest first, merged since —. Counts every MR, with no per-issue pick. Time spent in draft is excluded — the author is still working, not waiting on review. Note that an engineer working EET can score near zero here, since their own workday falls inside the Pacific night.

Filter is comma-separated and matches any part of a name. Add author searches GitLab as you type — pick a result, since merge requests are attributed by username and an email address matches nothing. Environment is read from the repo name, falling back to the changed file paths for repos like gitops-k8s-team-a2 that hold both trees; a change spanning both is counted as mixed and left out of the environment views. Otherwise — *-prod is production, *-nonprod is not, and shared repos like gitops-k8s-team-a2 are neither. Adding an author queues one full GitLab re-crawl, since their existing MRs are not in the store yet. Use × on a row to hide someone.

The same merged MRs, cut by the business-timezone day they landed rather than by author, over the lookback set at the top of the page and narrowed by the author filter above. Solid is the median, dashed the p90 — a day where the two diverge had one MR sitting, not a slow day. Hover a point for the exact figures. Days with nothing merged are omitted.

How these numbers are derived
Self-service vs AI-generated. A request is self-service when a skill-stamped label is present — pe-*, ai-generated or self-service on the Jira issue, or pe:<skill> on the linked MR. It is AI-generated when the MR description carries a Generated with Claude Code footer, whoever filed the ticket. They overlap but are not the same: the Jira labels only began 2026-05-27, two months after the first agent-footered MR, and 96 issues since then are footer-only — ordinary human-filed tickets PE delivered with the agent.
Business hours. Mon–Fri 08:00–17:00 US/Pacific, company holidays excluded, so a request filed 15:00 and closed 08:00 next morning is 2h, not 17h. A business day is 9h, a week 45h. Raw calendar time is not reported anywhere.
Delivery turnaround = created → first Done in the changelog (resolutiondate is null on ~85% of issues). MR turnaround = ready → merged: time in draft is excluded, because the author is still working rather than waiting on review. Across the 20 slowest MRs, 67% of attributed hours had been draft time.
Time to first review is the earliest of a non-author comment, a non-author approval, or a merge by someone other than the author. PE engineers merge their own work once a colleague approves, so a self-merge is not evidence nobody looked — the approval is the review. Requests from outside PE cannot be merged by the requester, so there the merge is the review.
Environment comes from the repo name, falling back to the changed file paths for repos like gitops-k8s-team-a2 that hold both trees. A change touching both is counted as mixed and left out of the environment views.
Population. Delivery-type issues only (Story/Task/Bug/Hotfix/Sub-task) — a Feature or Epic is a container for requests, not a request. Only merged MRs are ingested, so nothing here is a queue depth: an MR still open is invisible until it lands.
Windows. Impact cards and the monthly trend read the trailing 3 months of requests by creation date; MR turnaround keeps its own 6-month window back to 2026-03, when the workflows began. Both are overridden by the lookback control at the top.
Worth fixing. Roughly 18% of a typical request's turnaround falls after its MR merged — shipped work waiting to be closed out. An auto-transition on merge would improve every figure here for free (DEVOPS-10186).
